    West Midlands Pantomime & Festive Season 2026: The Ultimate Family Guide

    By Katy Rose Meaney

    Festive Magic Across the Midlands

    As autumn draws in, theatres across the West Midlands are preparing for the loudest, sparkliest, and most joyous time of the theatrical calendar. Whether your family thrives on classic dame banter, slapstick comedy, or spectacular scenic transformations, the 2026 festive season offers an extraordinary range of accessible, family-focused productions.

    For neurodivergent families and sensory-sensitive theatregoers, navigating the high-energy world of pantomime often requires careful planning. Fortunately, venues across our region are continuing to raise the bar with comprehensive access schedules, dedicated breakout spaces, and tailored sensory adjustments.

    Slapstick Royalty: The Big Stage Pantomimes

    Sleeping Beauty at Birmingham Hippodrome

    The Hippodrome panto remains an undisputed Midlands institution. This year, Birmingham pantomime legends Matt Slack and Andrew Ryan return to anchor Sleeping Beauty. Audiences can expect lavish sets, high-octane physical comedy, and show-stopping musical numbers. With a dedicated Relaxed Performance scheduled for late January alongside BSL, Captioned, and Audio Described dates, the Hippodrome ensures everyone can join in the festive cheer. A firm family favouritie at BTMC, Matt Slack has won us over time and again and we've witnessed first hand his "show must go on" approach, which makes even the cast howl with laughter. We can't wait to see what's in store this year! 

    Treasure Island at Wolverhampton Grand Theatre

    Fresh from clinching Best Panto in the UK at the UK Pantomime Awards, Wolverhampton Grand sets sail with a brand-new swashbuckling title: Treasure Island. Audience favourites Ian Adams and Tam Ryan team up once again for an original high-seas adventure. The Grand continues its stellar reputation for accessibility, programming three daytime relaxed performances featuring lowered sound volume, softened lighting cues, and calm breakout rooms. The lovable Tam Ryan has had us cry with laughter over the years, oh yes he has! We can't wait to see his swashbuckling adventure for 2026!

    Beauty and the Beast at Belgrade Theatre, Coventry

    Coventry’s favourite dame, Iain Lauchlan, returns to write and star in Beauty and the Beast. Known for brilliant local wit, eye-popping costumes, and family warmth, the Belgrade offers an exceptionally inclusive run—welcoming all ages with babes-in-arms lap tickets, booster seats, touch tours, and combined captioned/relaxed performances.

    Robin Hood at Lichfield Garrick

    The Lichfield Garrick is transforming into Sherwood Forest this winter as it prepares to stage Robin Hood. Marking the venue's first in-house-produced pantomime in over a decade, the festive spectacular arrives straight from the Garrick family to yours following a stellar milestone year that saw the venue produce its hit in-house musical Disenchanted! and secure its long-term future through an extended council lease.

    We love a home-grown festive treat here at Behind the Magic Curtain, and the 2026 line-up promises double the laughter, double the fun, and double the Ben! Garrick veteran Ben Thornton takes on the titular role of Robin Hood, joined by Great British Pantomime Award winner Ben Stock, who is ready to delight family audiences as the iconic Nurse Nellie. They are joined by Joe Feeney as the dastardly Sheriff of Nottingham, this production is set to be an unmissable festive highlight packed with traditional cheer, live music, and magical panto mischief.

    Aladdin at Stafford Gatehouse Theatre

    Over at Stafford Gatehouse, families can take flight on a magic carpet with Aladdin, starring Mark Rhodes, David Phipps-Davis, and Samantha Spragg. Packed with lively song-and-dance numbers, vibrant villains, and side-splitting physical comedy, the production features relaxed and signed performances in late December.

    The Gentle Magic Alternative: Peter Pan at The Rep

    While traditional pantomimes bring undeniable glitter and boisterous call-and-response fun, the shouting, strobe effects, and pyrotechnics can be sensory overload for many children. If your family is looking for genuine festive wonder and theatrical spectacle without the deafening "Behind you!" chaos, Peter Pan at Birmingham Rep is the standout festive alternative.

    Adapted by Phil Porter with an original score by Ruth Chan and directed by Joe Murphy, this faithful stage production of J.M. Barrie’s classic tale delivers breathless flying sequences, imaginative storytelling, and pirate mischief. It provides all the festive warmth and escapism of a seasonal outing within the gentler, immersive structure of a classic family play. The Rep backs this up with an exemplary access programme, including relaxed staging, touch tours, and captioned shows.
